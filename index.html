01Pass3 wks · Aug – Sep 2026 (+1 wk Mar 2027)
Course 1 · Pass · Real brief from TEDx Stockholm

CONNEC_TEDx

A service that helps TEDx attendees connect during and after the event: not another app, but an activation service plus small in-person gatherings. Honourable mention in the competition.

How it was built
  1. Survey

    74% of respondents valued meeting new people a lot. The barrier was not willingness, it was structure.

  2. Human needs

    We mapped what people need to open up: safety, belonging, autonomy, competence, contribution and meaning. "Help me find my people." "Give me a reason to contribute."

  3. Journey

    Before the event, share an intention. During, reflect on one idea. After, carry it forward with one next step.

  4. The loop

    Curiosity Club: small, curated gatherings in partner spaces like cafés and studios, so the end of one event is the start of the next.

  5. Access and cost

    Scan a QR code and go, no install. The model stays lean through TEDx access, partner venues and low-cost gatherings.

  6. Prototype

    A working mobile prototype of the journey, presented to TEDx Stockholm.

02Pass4 wks · Sep – Oct 2026
Course 2 · Pass · Student case study in a pair, not affiliated with Klarna

Smart Routing

Support that knows when to switch: AI for speed, humans for judgment. Money problems reach a specialist with the full history; simple cases stay with AI.

How it was built
  1. Context

    Klarna moved most support chats to AI, then started hiring human agents again as quality dropped. A new EU credit rule (CCD2) adds rights to human review from Nov 2026.

  2. Evidence

    We collected the 80 most recent 1-star Trustpilot reviews in Sweden, sorted them with AI and checked 10 by hand. 43 of 80 (54%) were money problems: returns and disputes, invoices and fees, failed payments.

  3. Reframe

    Our first idea was to explain declined credit to young customers. Only 2 of 80 reviews were about that, so we changed the problem: get a customer with a money problem to someone who can explain and fix it.

  4. Decide

    Three ideas: Credit Boost, Choose AI or human, Smart Routing. On impact × effort, Smart Routing came first (impact 5, effort 2).

  5. Feedback

    A Klarna customer warned that sending every money question to a human could cost almost as much as before. We kept all money cases in scope to protect trust, and made cost a KPI to watch.

  6. Pilot plan

    Seven weeks: baselines and rules, a pilot on returns and disputes, then go, change or stop. KPIs: money cases solved on first contact, cost per case, retention after six months, and money complaints against the 54% baseline.

03Pass with distinction7 wks · Oct – Nov 2026
Course 3 · Pass with distinction · Student project, team of five

Rollio

Helps people choose a movie: answer a few short questions, get a small list from your own streaming services.

How it was built
  1. Week 1 · Problem

    Ranked many ideas by value and difficulty and picked "what to watch": real friction we could observe and room to iterate.

  2. Week 2 · Research

    Survey of 29 people and 14 interviews. 26 of 29 said mood shaped the choice; 22 of 29 decide within ten minutes. The goal moved from "choose faster" to "picks that fit the moment".

  3. Week 3 · Plan

    Opportunity tree and story map. Riskiest assumption: will users give a little context for a more relevant shortlist?

  4. Week 4 · Release 1

    Mood, time and company give 3 to 5 picks. Less effort, but trust in the picks was weak.

  5. Week 5 · Release 2, split

    About 34 story points was too big, so we split it: clearer questions and richer info first, then streaming filters, real posters and refresh without restarting.

  6. Week 6 · Final tests

    7 of 8 users said Rollio was faster than how they usually choose. Release 3 was planned, not built: explain why a title fits and mark "already watched".

Course 4 · In progress · Student group project, team of four, not affiliated with SL

SL app · Feature strategy

Decide what the SL app, Stockholm's public transport app, should be great at over the next 18 months. Due 3 Dec: a two-page strategy, three features for the next six months and a plan by quarter.

How it is going
  1. Point of departure

    I prepared our team charter: purpose, roles, ways of working, milestones, and what success and failure look like. I also set up the kickoff on our FigJam board.

  2. Four lenses

    Market (me), business, customer and data. Together they answer one question: what should the app be great at, and why?

  3. Evidence first

    Every claim needs a number, a rider quote or a source. We score ideas on how often the problem happens, how painful it is, how weak the alternative is and the value for SL.

  4. My research

    I compare the SL app with Google Maps, Apple Maps, Res i STHLM and tap-to-pay at the gate, look for what only SL can offer and benchmark TfL Go, Ruter, Skånetrafiken and Citymapper. My output is a first positioning: "The SL app should be great at…, because…".

  5. Next

    Explore on 25 Nov, decide on 27 Nov, write from 30 Nov, submit on 2 Dec. Results here after the presentation.

Story

.05

I was born in 1992 in Medeiros, a small town in Minas Gerais, Brazil, and left home at 15 to study. I earned a full scholarship to study Civil Engineering. Engineering gave me a methodical, fact-based way of seeing the world, but I have always cared just as much about creativity and the things that make users' eyes light up. The best work, for me, is rigorous and still creates emotion and connection with the people who use it.

AIESEC showed me how the world works and taught me empathy. I joined as a volunteer in 2015 and grew to its national board.

In 2021 I married my Italian husband, and in 2022 we moved to Sweden for his job. Starting over built resilience: I learned English and Italian, and worked in a pizzeria, at a Brazilian market, in internships and at a Swedish company.
